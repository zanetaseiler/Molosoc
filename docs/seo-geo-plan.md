# Molosoc — SEO + GEO Content Architecture Plan

**Purpose of this document:** a single reference for planning article keywords and content across the whole site. Combines the ICP, the brand architecture, and the site map worked out together, so nothing has to be re-derived later.

---

## 1. Product definition

Molosoc is a **reusable moisture-lock foot treatment cover** — not a sock, not a pre-filled mask, not a medical device. It traps a cream against the skin, cuts the mess, and makes an existing foot-care routine actually get finished. It works *with* whatever cream the customer already owns — a companion product, not a replacement.

**Message hierarchy (always in this order):**
1. Locks in moisture
2. Reduces mess and slipping
3. Works with your favorite cream
4. Easy self-care ritual for soft, cared-for feet

**Brand promise:**
> Molosoc helps you actually follow through on foot care — not just start it.

**The core reframe:** target a **broken routine**, not a demographic. Most of the audience is already sold on foot care mattering — Molosoc's job is conversion (finish the routine), not education (convince them feet matter). Gender/age are downstream of behavior, not the starting filter.

## 2. The three-layer site architecture

```
BRAND (Molosoc / Homepage)     → owns the SUBJECT: foot care, broadly
  └─ CATEGORY (Category page)   → owns a SOLUTION TYPE: reusable / moisture-lock foot covers
       └─ PRODUCT (Product page) → owns a SKU: Molosoc Foot Cover
```

Each layer has a different content job and a different SEO target. Never let brand-layer content try to rank on category-layer keywords, or vice versa.

| Layer | Owns | Content job | Primary SEO target |
|---|---|---|---|
| Homepage | Subject: foot care | Brand belief, ritual, "why it matters," topical authority | "foot care" (territory/voice, not a head-term ranking battle) |
| Category page | Solution type: reusable / moisture-lock covers | Argue format (reusable vs. disposable), explain mechanism, objection-handling | `foot covers`, `reusable foot covers`, `moisture-lock foot cover` (owned descriptor) |
| Product page | SKU: Molosoc Foot Cover | Persona-specific proof and conversion | Long-tail, persona/proof-intent keywords |

**Homepage job specifics:**
- Present brand promise + pillars front and center
- Represent "foot care" as the broad subject via articles/tips/the "why" — not narrowed to one SKU's mechanism
- Introduce the Foot Cover as "how Molosoc helps right now," not "what Molosoc is"
- Leave room for the brand to expand beyond one product later

**Category page job specifics:**
- Argue reusable + cream-agnostic vs. disposable pre-filled masks
- Explain the mechanism
- Carry the objection-handling content (see §5)

**Product page job specifics:**
- Conversion-intent, long-tail SEO
- Specific proof/objection content matched to persona (see §4–5)

**Future naming convention (branded house model):** `Molosoc + [Body Part] Cover` — Foot Cover (now), Hand Cover / Heel Cover / Elbow Cover (future). URL pattern: `molosoc.com/foot-cover`, `molosoc.com/hand-cover`, etc., nested under the category page.

## 3. Category-layer keyword data (for reference when planning category-page and category-supporting content)

| Term | Vol/mo | CPC | Role |
|---|---|---|---|
| `foot covers` | 3,600 | $0.13 | SEO head term — real volume, must be qualified fast (title/H1) to avoid overlap with PPE/shoe-cover/rain-cover intent |
| `reusable foot covers` | long-tail, low but qualified | — | Primary on-page target — narrower, better-intent phrasing |
| `moisture lock` | 590 | $0.28 | Supporting phrase, generic outside foot-care — use in subheads/copy, not as sole target |
| `moisture-lock foot cover` | 0 | $0.00 | Ownable brand-category descriptor — 0 volume today (doesn't exist as a search term yet); build equity over time through consistent on-page use, the way "gua sha" or "jade roller" became searchable only after brand-driven repetition |
| `lock cover`, `moisture proof` | 1,300 / 720 | — | Discard — broad-match noise from unrelated categories (locks, packaging, electronics) |

**Market context:** foot care is a large, growing category (~$11.46B global market 2025 per Grand View Research; foot mask sub-market ~$565.2M 2025 per IMARC). Czech retail (DM, Rossmann, Dr. Max, Notino) is dominated by disposable, pre-filled, single-use sock masks. Molosoc's white space: reusable + cream-agnostic + less mess + home ritual.

## 4. The four personas

### Persona 1 — The Cream Graveyard Owner
Has 2–3 half-used foot creams/balms in a bathroom drawer. Bought each with real intent, used it a handful of times, stopped — not because it didn't work, but because there was no structure to keep using it.
- **Message:** "The cream you already own, finally working."
- **Content type:** show the mess/inconsistency problem visually — don't explain the product, show the friction it removes.
- **Value:** highest-conversion, lowest-education-cost buyer.

### Persona 2 — The Ingrown-Nail / Hardened-Skin Sufferer
Actual physical problem: ingrown nails, blackened/hardened skin around nails, cracked heels. In pain or shame, actively searching for a fix. Converts on proof, not aspiration.
- **Message:** real before/after, time-stamped, "no filters."
- **Content type:** existing proof-case style (3-month testimonial, mom-reaction video) — strongest performing format.

### Persona 3 — The After-Pedicure Maintainer
Just had a salon pedicure/exfoliation, wants to extend the result instead of watching it fade in ~10 days. Price-conscious about repeat salon visits.
- **Message:** "Make the pedicure last."
- **Content type:** cost-per-use math, reusable vs. disposable framing.

### Persona 4 — The Gift Buyer
Buying for someone "always on her feet" or "never takes care of herself." Converts on ease of gifting and one clear visual (cracked → smooth), not mechanism explanation.
- **Message:** simple, visual, low-effort gifting story.
- **Content type:** one strong before/after image or clip, minimal explanation.

## 5. Objections by persona (content angles)

| Persona | Core objection | Content response |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Cream Graveyard | "I'll just use my cream normally" | Show the mess/inconsistency problem visually |
| 2. Pain/Shame | "Does this actually work or is it a gimmick" | Before/after, time-stamped, unfiltered proof |
| 3. Maintainer | "Isn't this more expensive than just going to the salon" | Cost-per-use, reusable vs. disposable math |
| 4. Gift | "Will she actually use it" | Simplicity/ease framing, not mechanism |

## 6. Seasonality calendar

**Key insight:** summer is the peak *exposure* window, not the peak *intent* window — don't conflate them.

| Window | Months | Intent level | Angle |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pre-summer prep | April–June | Real peak window | "Get sandal-ready" urgency |
| Deep summer | July–August | Intent drops (travel, vacation, routines collapse) | Do NOT run "it's summer, start a routine" content — underperforms |
| Post-summer repair | Aug–Sept | Second real spike | "Summer wrecked my feet, here's the fix" outperforms "get ready for summer" — matches best-performing testimonial content |
| Winter habit-building | Oct–March | Lowest exposure pressure, best habit window | Private, cozy, "just for me" ritual — ideal for Persona 1; socks/tights dryness is an underused angle |

**Rule of thumb:** run two urgency spikes (pre-summer prep, post-summer repair) + one habit-building spike (winter ritual) — not one flat "summer = the moment" push.

Seasonal content sits at the **product/conversion layer** (it's driving a purchase decision, timed to a moment), not the category layer (which stays evergreen).

## 7. Full content map — what supports each layer

This is the structure agreed on for planning articles. Four content blocks total, three tied to the three site layers plus one top-of-funnel block that sits under the homepage but funnels down to the product page.

### 7a. Awareness layer (supports Homepage)
**Job:** brand belief, "foot care" as broad subject, topical authority. Not product mechanism, not proof.
**Content types:** social media, PR/press releases, brand-story articles ("why foot care matters," the broken-routine narrative).

### 7b. Problem-education layer — TOFU (also sits under Homepage, funnels to Product)
**Job:** answer broad, high-volume problem searches from people who don't yet know a solution category exists. No format argument, no product mention in the title. Builds the brand's claim on "foot care" as subject and is a strong GEO surface (these are the pages most likely to get pulled into an AI-generated answer).
**Internal linking:** links straight down to the Product page's Persona 2 proof content — skips the category page, because someone with an actual cracked-heel problem needs proof it works, not the reusable-vs-disposable argument first.
**Example article titles (to build keyword lists from):**
- "How to heal cracked heels naturally"
- "Why do heels crack in winter (and how to stop it)"
- "How to treat dry, flaky feet at home"
- "Ingrown toenail vs. hardened skin: what's actually causing the pain"
- "Home remedies for hardened skin around the nails"

### 7c. Consideration layer (supports Category page)
**Job:** argue the format — reusable vs. disposable, mechanism, cost-per-use. This is where Persona 3's objection lives.
**Content types:** "reusable vs. disposable foot masks" comparisons, "what does moisture-lock actually mean" definitional content (building the ownable descriptor), cost-per-use math articles.

### 7d. Conversion layer (supports Product page)
**Job:** persona-specific proof and timing, matched to purchase decision.
**Content types:** Persona 2 before/after testimonials, Persona 4 gift-guide content, Persona 1 "cream you already own" angle, seasonal content (pre-summer prep, post-summer repair, winter ritual — see §6).

### Layer relationship at a glance

| Layer | Feeds | Keyword character | Buying intent |
|---|---|---|---|
| Awareness (homepage) | Brand belief | Broad, non-commercial | None |
| Problem-education (TOFU, homepage) | → Product page directly | High-volume, problem-based ("how to heal X") | Low, but highest GEO value |
| Consideration (category) | Category page | Format/comparison, `reusable foot covers`, `moisture-lock foot cover` | Medium |
| Conversion (product) | Product page | Persona + proof + seasonal, long-tail | High |

## 8. How to apply this when planning keywords

1. Sort every candidate article into one of the four content blocks in §7 before researching keywords for it — the block determines whether you're chasing volume (TOFU, category head-term) or intent-match (product, persona proof).
2. For the category layer, treat `foot covers` / `reusable foot covers` as the rankable SEO target and `moisture-lock foot cover` as the descriptor to build consistently in copy even without current volume.
3. For TOFU articles, prioritize clear, quotable, structured problem/solution writing — this is the layer most likely to surface in AI-generated answers (GEO), and it should link down to product proof content rather than the category page.
4. For product-layer articles, match content to a specific persona (§4) and, where seasonal, to the correct window (§6) — don't default to generic "summer" framing.
5. Keep the message hierarchy consistent everywhere: mess/consistency problem solved > works with existing cream > easy ritual > soft feet result.
