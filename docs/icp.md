# Molosoc — Ideal Customer Profile (ICP)
**Use this as standing context for all Molosoc content, marketing, and strategy work.**

---

## 1. Product definition (for context)

Molosoc is a **reusable moisture-lock foot treatment cover** — not a sock, not a pre-filled mask, not a medical device. It's the accessory that traps a cream against the skin, cuts the mess, and makes an existing foot-care routine actually get finished. It works *with* whatever cream the customer already owns — it is a companion product, not a replacement.

Core message hierarchy:
1. Locks in moisture
2. Reduces mess and slipping
3. Works with your favorite cream
4. Easy self-care ritual for soft, cared-for feet

---

## 2. The core reframe (do not default to this without reading it)

**Old approach:** target "women," broadly, as a demographic.
**Correct approach:** target a **broken routine**, not a demographic. The customer is whoever already owns a foot cream, uses it inconsistently, and would keep using it if the mess/effort/forgetting problem disappeared. Most of the ICP is already sold on foot care mattering — Molosoc's job is conversion (finish the routine), not education (convince them feet matter).

Gender, age, and other demographics are downstream of behavior, not the starting filter. Some personas below skew female by behavior (pedicure culture, cream ownership) but are not exclusively female — e.g., a man with cracked heels from standing all day at work is a legitimate, currently-unaddressed buyer.

---

## 3. The four personas

### Persona 1 — The Cream Graveyard Owner
Has 2–3 half-used foot creams/balms sitting in a bathroom drawer right now. Bought each one with real intent, used it a handful of times, stopped — not because it didn't work, but because there was no structure to keep using it. Doesn't need convincing foot care matters. Needs a reason the habit sticks this time.
- **Message:** "The cream you already own, finally working."
- **Content type:** show the mess/inconsistency problem visually — don't explain the product, show the friction it removes.
- **Value:** highest-conversion, lowest-education-cost buyer.

### Persona 2 — The Ingrown-Nail / Hardened-Skin Sufferer
Has an actual physical problem: ingrown nails needing scissors, blackened/hardened skin around nails, cracked heels. In pain or shame, actively searching for a fix. Converts on proof, not aspiration.
- **Message:** real before/after, time-stamped, "no filters."
- **Content type:** this is the existing proof-case content style (3-month testimonial, mom-reaction video) — keep leaning here, it's the strongest performing format.

### Persona 3 — The After-Pedicure Maintainer
Just had a salon pedicure/exfoliation and wants to extend the result instead of watching it fade in ~10 days. Price-conscious about repeat salon visits; wants "home spa without the salon cost."
- **Message:** "Make the pedicure last."
- **Content type:** cost-per-use math, reusable vs. disposable framing.

### Persona 4 — The Gift Buyer
Buying for a mother, partner, or friend who "is always on her feet" or "never takes care of herself." Converts on ease of gifting and one clear visual (cracked → smooth), not on mechanism explanation.
- **Message:** simple, visual, low-effort gifting story.
- **Content type:** one strong before/after image or clip, minimal explanation.

## 4. Objections by persona (for content angles)

| Persona | Core objection | Content response |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Cream Graveyard | "I'll just use my cream normally" | Show the mess/inconsistency problem visually |
| 2. Pain/Shame | "Does this actually work or is it a gimmick" | Before/after, time-stamped, unfiltered proof |
| 3. Maintainer | "Isn't this more expensive than just going to the salon" | Cost-per-use, reusable vs. disposable math |
| 4. Gift | "Will she actually use it" | Simplicity/ease framing, not mechanism |

## 5. Seasonality — the summer correction

**Key insight: summer is the peak *exposure* window, not the peak *intent* window. These are different and must not be conflated.**

- **Pre-summer (April–June) — real peak window.** Sandals coming, feet about to be seen, shame/motivation highest. "Get sandal-ready" urgency messaging belongs here.
- **Deep summer (July–August) — intent actually drops.** Travel, vacation chaos, skincare routines generally collapse in heat. Feet are already in flip-flops, so visible urgency is lower. **Do not run "it's summer, start a routine" content here — it underperforms.**
- **Late summer/early fall (Aug–Sept) — second real spike.** Damage from a summer of sandals, chlorine, hot pavement, barefoot walking shows up now. "Summer wrecked my feet, here's the fix" outperforms "get ready for summer." This matches existing best-performing testimonial content.
- **Winter (Oct–March) — lowest exposure pressure, best habit-building window.** Feet are hidden, so this is a private, cozy, "just for me" ritual play — ideal for Persona 1 (Cream Graveyard). Socks/tights dryness is a real, underused angle here.

**Rule of thumb:** run two urgency spikes (pre-summer prep, post-summer repair) + one habit-building spike (winter ritual), instead of one flat "summer = the moment" push.

## 6. Market context (from prior strategic report — for reference)

- Foot care is a large, growing category (global foot care products market ~$11.46B in 2025 per Grand View Research; foot mask sub-market ~$565.2M in 2025 per IMARC) — the market has already trained consumers to want foot treatment.
- Czech retail (DM, Rossmann, Dr. Max, Notino) is dominated by **disposable, pre-filled, single-use sock masks**. Molosoc's white space is: **reusable + cream-agnostic + less mess + home ritual.**
- Molosoc should be positioned as the companion step that makes an existing routine work, not a replacement for cream or a competitor to disposable masks.

---

## 7. How to apply this in every future chat

When writing content, ads, captions, or strategy for Molosoc:
1. Identify which of the 4 personas the piece is speaking to — never write for "everyone."
2. Identify which seasonal window applies (pre-summer prep / deep-summer-skip / post-summer repair / winter habit) — don't default to generic "summer" framing.
3. Match the objection-response pairing above to the content angle.
4. Keep the message hierarchy: mess/consistency problem solved > works with existing cream > easy ritual > soft feet result.
