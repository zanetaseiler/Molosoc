# Molosoc — Brand Architecture & SEO Structure
**Use this as standing context alongside `molosoc_icp_instructions.md` for all Molosoc brand, homepage, category, product, and SEO work.**

---

## 1. The core structure — three layers, do not collapse them

```
BRAND (Molosoc)          → owns the SUBJECT: foot care, broadly
  └─ CATEGORY (page)      → owns a SOLUTION TYPE: reusable / moisture-lock foot covers
       └─ PRODUCT (page)  → owns a SKU: Molosoc Foot Cover
```

Each layer has a different job. Content, copy, and SEO targeting should always be written with awareness of which layer it's serving.

---

## 2. Layer 1 — Brand (Molosoc) = Homepage

**Molosoc is a foot care brand, not "the cover site."** The cover is today's flagship product; foot care is the permanent subject the brand lives in. This means the brand can outlive and outgrow the current single SKU.

**Brand promise:**
> Molosoc helps you actually follow through on foot care — not just start it.

This is the elevated, durable version of the ICP reframe: the target isn't a demographic, it's a *broken routine*. Every persona (Cream Graveyard, Ingrown-Nail Sufferer, After-Pedicure Maintainer, Gift Buyer) already believes feet matter — Molosoc's job is conversion (finish the routine), not education.

**Brand pillars:**
1. Feet deserve real care, not just intent
2. Care should be simple enough to actually stick
3. You don't need new products, you need the right accessory/ritual to use what you already have
4. Foot care is self-care, not vanity

**Tone:** warm, non-clinical, encouraging. Speaks to someone who already tried and stopped — not someone who needs convincing.

**Homepage job:**
- Present the brand promise + pillars front and center
- Represent "foot care" as the broad subject — via articles, tips, the "why" — not narrowed to one SKU's mechanism
- Introduce the Foot Cover as "how Molosoc helps right now," not "what Molosoc is"
- Leave visible room for the brand to expand beyond one product later
- Link out into Category and Product pages, which carry the sharper commercial SEO weight

**Homepage keyword role:** brand can claim "foot care" as *subject/territory* in voice and content — but should not expect to win a head-term ranking battle against podiatry/medical/salon sites on day one. That competitive reality is handled structurally by the category layer below, not by avoiding the subject.

---

## 3. Layer 2 — Category (Category page)

This is where product-type specificity and the real, winnable SEO targeting live.

**Category concept:** reusable / moisture-lock foot covers — the alternative to disposable, pre-filled, single-use sock masks (the dominant format at DM, Rossmann, Dr. Max, Notino). White space = reusable + cream-agnostic + less mess + home ritual.

**Keyword strategy (two-layer, based on actual data):**

| Term | Vol/mo | CPC | Role |
|---|---|---|---|
| `foot covers` | 3,600 | $0.13 | **SEO head term** — real volume, but must be qualified fast (title/H1) to avoid overlap with PPE/shoe-cover/rain-cover search intent |
| `reusable foot covers` | (long-tail, low but qualified) | — | **Primary on-page target** — narrower, better-intent phrasing built from the head term |
| `moisture lock` | 590 | $0.28 | Supporting phrase, generic outside foot-care — use in subheads/copy, not as sole target |
| `moisture-lock foot cover` | 0 | $0.00 | **Ownable brand-category descriptor** — 0 volume today because it doesn't exist yet as a search term; build equity in it over time through consistent on-page use, the way "gua sha" or "jade roller" became searchable only after brand-driven repetition |
| `lock cover`, `moisture proof` | 1,300 / 720 | — | **Discard** — broad-match noise from unrelated categories (locks, packaging, electronics), not real candidates |

**Rule:** target `foot covers` / `reusable foot covers` for rankable SEO traffic; use `moisture-lock foot cover` consistently as the brand's owned descriptor in copy, naming, and voice, even while it has no search volume yet. Both are correct at the same time, at different layers.

**Category page job:** argue reusable + cream-agnostic vs. disposable pre-filled masks; explain the mechanism; carry the objection-handling content (see ICP doc §4).

---

## 4. Layer 3 — Product (Product page)

**Current SKU:** Molosoc Foot Cover.

**Naming convention for future expansion (branded house model):** `Molosoc + [Body Part] Cover`
- Molosoc Foot Cover *(now)*
- Molosoc Hand Cover *(future)*
- Molosoc Heel Cover / Elbow Cover *(future, targeted variants)*

This lets every future SKU inherit Molosoc's domain authority and search equity instead of starting a new brand name from zero. URL pattern: `molosoc.com/foot-cover`, `molosoc.com/hand-cover`, etc., all nested under the category page.

**Product page job:** conversion-intent, long-tail SEO, specific proof/objection content matched to persona (see ICP doc §3–4).

---

## 5. Quick-reference decision rule

Before writing any Molosoc page, ask: **which of the three layers is this?**
- Talking about *why foot care matters, ritual, brand belief* → Brand/homepage layer, "foot care" is the subject
- Talking about *reusable vs. disposable, the mechanism, moisture-lock* → Category layer, "reusable foot covers" is the SEO target, "moisture-lock foot cover" is the descriptor to build
- Talking about *this specific item, specs, proof, price* → Product layer, persona-specific objection handling

Never write brand-layer content trying to rank on category-layer keywords, or vice versa — that's what causes the "foot care" vs. "foot cover" confusion to bleed into everything.
