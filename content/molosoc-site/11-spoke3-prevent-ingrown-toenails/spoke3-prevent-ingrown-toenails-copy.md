## H1: How to prevent ingrown toenails

If this isn't the first time, treating the current episode isn't enough — preventing the next one means changing whatever keeps recreating the same pressure point.

---

## H2: Why do I keep getting ingrown toenails?

### H3: The nail-cutting habit that causes repeat problems
Cutting the nail too short or rounding the corners gives the same edge room to dig into skin again every time it regrows.

### H3: Footwear that keeps recreating the same pressure point
Shoes that press against the same spot on the toe recreate the exact pressure that caused the problem the first time, every time they're worn.

### H3: Why one successful treatment doesn't mean it won't happen again
Resolving one episode deals with the symptom in front of you — it doesn't change the trim habit or footwear that caused it, so the same conditions are still in place for next time.

---

## H2: How to stop ingrown toenails

### H3: The trim shape that actually prevents regrowth into skin
Trimming straight across, leaving the corners slightly longer than the center, gives the nail a straighter path to grow along instead of curving into skin.

### H3: Shoe-fit changes that remove the root pressure
Shoes with enough room at the toe box remove the constant pressure that pushes the nail edge into skin in the first place.

### H3: Softening the surrounding skin as an ongoing habit, not a one-time fix
Keeping the skin around the nail consistently soft, rather than only after a problem starts, gives the nail room to grow past it instead of into it.

---

## H2: Building a prevention routine that actually sticks

### H3: Why prevention fails the same way treatment does — no structure
Prevention habits fall off for the same reason treatment routines do — there's no structure keeping them going once the immediate problem has passed.

### H3: What a five-minute weekly check actually catches
A quick weekly look at trim shape, shoe fit, and skin condition catches small issues before they become a repeat episode.

### H3: Making it part of an existing routine instead of a new one
Attaching this check to something already happening regularly — a shower, a nail-trim day — makes it far more likely to actually continue.
