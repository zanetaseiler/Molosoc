## H1: Does foot cream actually work?

Foot cream works — the reason it often seems like it doesn't is that most of it never gets the chance to actually absorb before it rubs off.

---

## H2: Why creams get abandoned halfway through

### H3: Real intent, no structure to keep going
Most people buy a foot cream with real intent to use it regularly — the intent isn't the part that runs out first.

### H3: The mess that makes people stop reaching for it
Greasy sheets, slippery floors, and socks that won't stay on are usually the actual reason someone quietly stops reaching for a cream they still believe in.

### H3: It's not that the cream failed
A half-used bottle in the drawer usually isn't evidence the formula didn't work — it's evidence the routine around it didn't hold.

---

## H2: How long does foot cream take to absorb?

### H3: Why timing matters more than the cream itself
Most creams need sustained, uninterrupted contact with skin to fully absorb — often longer than the few minutes most routines actually give them before socks or shoes go back on.

### H3: Should you put socks on after foot cream
Socks after cream help hold moisture against skin longer than bare feet do, which is part of why the classic "cream plus socks" combination works better than cream alone.

### H3: How to get soft feet overnight
The traditional approach is a heavier cream sealed under socks overnight, giving skin hours of uninterrupted contact to absorb. A shorter, focused session with the cream sealed against skin can get much of that same result without needing to commit to wearing anything all night.

---

## H2: The cream graveyard problem

### H3: Why bathrooms end up with 2-3 half-used bottles
Each bottle usually got bought with real intent, used a handful of times, then stalled — not from one bad experience, but from the same mess-and-mid-routine drop-off repeating with every new cream.

### H3: What that pattern actually says about the routine, not the product
Buying a new cream doesn't fix the actual gap, which is structure — the same drop-off tends to happen again with whatever's bought next.

### H3: Breaking the cycle without buying a new cream
The fix isn't another bottle — it's giving the cream already owned a real, sealed session to actually work, instead of replacing it before it's had the chance.
