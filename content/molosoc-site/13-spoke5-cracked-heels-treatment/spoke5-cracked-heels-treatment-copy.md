## H1: Cracked heels treatment: what actually works

Real cracked-heel treatment is three things done together — removing built-up dead skin, getting moisture in, and keeping it sealed there — not cream applied on its own.

---

## H2: The three things actual treatment requires

### H3: Removing the built-up dead skin first
Thickened, dead skin has to come off first, since cream applied over it has a much harder surface to actually penetrate.

### H3: Getting moisture in, not just on
Moisture needs to reach into the skin, not just sit on the surface — which is why application alone, without anything holding it there, often underperforms.

### H3: The step most routines skip entirely — sealing it in
Sealing the cream against skin for a real session is the step most routines skip, and it's usually the single biggest difference between a routine that works and one that doesn't.

---

## H2: When to see a podiatrist instead of treating it yourself

### H3: Signs a crack has gone past the surface layer
Sharp pain, visible depth, or a crack that doesn't respond to a week or two of consistent care are signs it's gone past what home treatment alone typically resolves.

### H3: Painful deep cracked heels
Deep, painful cracks — the kind that hurt with every step — are exactly the point where it's worth involving a podiatrist rather than continuing to self-treat.

### H3: Why bleeding or infection changes the plan
Bleeding, discharge, or signs of infection change the situation entirely and call for professional care rather than continued home treatment.

---

## H2: Building a treatment routine that actually finishes

### H3: Why most people stop after the first sign of improvement
The moment cracked heels start feeling better, the motivation to keep going drops — which is exactly when the underlying skin is still recovering and most likely to relapse.

### H3: What locking moisture in actually changes by the next morning
A single sealed session, done properly, can leave skin visibly softer by the next morning — a clear, early sign that consistent sessions add up over time.

### H3: Making it a five-minute nightly step, not a project
The actual effort is about five minutes — applying cream and sealing it in. The results build from doing that small step consistently, not from turning it into a bigger routine than it needs to be.
