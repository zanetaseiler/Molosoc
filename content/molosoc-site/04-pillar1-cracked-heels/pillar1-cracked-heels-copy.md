## H1: Cracked heels: what's really going on, and what helps

Cracked heels happen when skin loses the elasticity to stretch under pressure — not from bad luck, and not from "just dry skin." The fix is straightforward once it's consistent, not occasional.

---

## H2: What causes cracked heels

### H3: Dry skin losing its ability to stretch
Heel skin cracks when it dries out and loses the elasticity it needs to stretch under pressure.

### H3: Pressure and weight-bearing on the heel edge
The heel's outer edge carries the most weight with every step — dry, inflexible skin can't take that pressure without splitting.

### H3: Standing all day, especially in open-back shoes
Standing for long stretches in open-back shoes speeds up both problems at once: more pressure, more moisture loss.

---

## H2: When cracked heels go from dry to painful

Not every cracked heel is the same — severity changes what it actually needs.

### H3: Painful deep cracked heels
A crack that hurts with every step has gone past surface dryness into a deeper layer of skin.

### H3: Severe cracked heels
Severe cracked heels widen, deepen, and can bleed — an occasional cream application isn't enough at this stage.

### H3: Why "just moisturizing more" stops working at this stage
Cream that isn't sealed against skin rubs off before it's absorbed — more product doesn't fix that, more contact time does.

---

## H2: Why moisturizer alone doesn't fix it

### H3: The routine most people already try
Most people already own a foot cream and already apply it — the routine itself was never the problem.

### H3: Where that routine breaks down
Cream rubs off onto socks or sheets within minutes, so most of it never gets absorbed.

### H3: What actually needs to happen for cream to work
Cream needs uninterrupted contact with skin to work — not a bigger dose, just enough sealed time to actually absorb.
