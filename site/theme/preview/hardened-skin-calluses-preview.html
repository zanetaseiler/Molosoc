Hardened skin & calluses

Hardened skin and calluses: why they form, and what softens them

Hardened skin builds up gradually as skin's response to repeated pressure and friction — and it softens the same way it formed: gradually, with consistent care.

The causes

Calluses on feet

Foot callus

A foot callus is an area of thickened, hardened skin that forms where pressure and friction repeat in the same spot, most often the ball of the foot or the heel.

Corns and calluses

Corns are smaller, more concentrated areas of hardened skin, usually on or between toes; calluses cover broader, flatter areas — both form for the same reason, just in different places.

Why it builds up gradually, not overnight

Skin thickens in layers over weeks of repeated pressure, which is why it never appears suddenly and why softening it also takes sustained time, not a single treatment.

When it gets serious

Foot corn cure

Callus treatment

Effective callus treatment softens the thickened skin gradually with consistent moisture, rather than removing it all at once.

When thick skin starts affecting how you walk

Once hardened skin gets thick enough, it changes how weight distributes across the foot with each step, which is a sign it's past the point of ignoring.

Why picking or cutting it off yourself is risky

Cutting or picking at hardened skin can go deeper than intended and leave skin exposed and vulnerable — softening it is a safer route to the same result.
