Ingrown toenails

Ingrown toenails: what's happening, and what actually helps

An ingrown toenail happens when the nail edge grows into the surrounding skin instead of over it — and it's rarely a one-time event without a reason behind it.

The causes

What causes ingrown toenails

How the nail edge starts growing into skin

An ingrown toenail starts when the nail's edge curves or grows down into the skin beside it instead of straight across.

Tight shoes and cutting nails too short

Tight-fitting shoes press the skin against the nail edge, and nails trimmed too short or rounded give that edge room to dig in as it grows back.

Why some people get them again and again

The same nail shape and the same shoe habits keep recreating the same pressure point — so without a change to either, it tends to repeat.

When it gets serious

Do ingrown toenails go away

When it resolves without intervention

A mild ingrown toenail can resolve on its own once the pressure causing it — tight shoes, a too-short trim — is removed and the skin has room to heal.

When it doesn't — signs it's getting worse

Increasing redness, swelling, or pain that doesn't ease up after a few days means it isn't resolving on its own.

Why waiting too long makes it harder to treat

The longer skin stays irritated and under pressure, the more inflamed and sensitive it gets — which makes it harder for anything applied to it to actually help.

Ingrown big toe

The big toe is the most common site for an ingrown nail simply because it takes the most pressure with every step.

What softening the surrounding skin actually changes

Softer, healthier skin around the nail gives the nail edge room to grow past it instead of into it — which is the actual mechanism behind fewer repeat episodes.
